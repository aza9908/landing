AI in Food Service: From Orders to Analytics with ORDA Platform

Azamat Armanuly M.S. KAIST · AI Research Labs
Jul 10, 2026 · 8 min read

Food service is one of the most operationally complex industries: dozens of SKUs, shifts, suppliers, write-offs, seasonality. Here, artificial intelligence isn't about a “customer chatbot” — it's about end-to-end analytics and operations automation. ORDA Platform is a live case of how this works in practice.

The problem: plenty of data, no decisions

A typical coffee shop or restaurant chain is already collecting data: the POS system, inventory, purchasing, staff schedules. But it all lives in Excel, Telegram chats and managers' heads. Decisions are made on gut feeling: “Let's order more milk — we ran short last time.”

ORDA Platform solves this with a single operations dashboard backed by a data warehouse. A demo is available at timely-beignet-9d8277.netlify.app — a simulated environment that shows what running an entire chain from one screen looks like.

What ORDA does

  • Operations dashboard — revenue, average check and location traffic in real time
  • Inventory & procurement — purchasing forecasts based on sales history and seasonality
  • Staff scheduling — shift optimization that accounts for peak hours
  • Data warehouse — a single data layer for analytics and ML models

Where the AI comes in

The first level is predictive analytics: the system forecasts ingredient demand and suggests how much to order. The second is anomaly detection: if a location's revenue drops sharply, ORDA alerts the manager before the problem becomes critical.

The third level is generative AI for reporting: instead of crunching numbers in Excel by hand, the manager gets a written summary of “what changed this week and why” — in the language of business, not SQL.

“We don't teach food service businesses how to use ChatGPT. We build AI into their operational processes — where it delivers measurable results: less waste, more accurate purchasing, faster decisions.”

Case study: a 12-location chain

In a pilot project, a coffee chain connected ORDA to its POS and inventory system. In the first month: write-offs fell by 18%, the time spent on the weekly report dropped from 4 hours to 20 minutes, and automatic alerts flagged 3 anomalies that previously would only have been caught after the fact.
