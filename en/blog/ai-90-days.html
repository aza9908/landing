90 Days to Your Company's First AI Result

AI
AI Research Labs Applied AI center
2026 · 6 min read

Most corporate AI initiatives die at the “pilot for the sake of a pilot” stage: a slick demo, an enthusiastic presentation — and zero change to the P&L. This roadmap is about reaching a measurable result in 90 days, not producing yet another slide.

Days 1–30: pick a process, not a technology

The first month isn't about models — it's about processes. Map your routine operations and sort them along two axes: how many hours a month each process eats up, and how well-defined its inputs and outputs are. Your first candidate for automation is a high-volume process with a clear structure: request handling, first-line support, report preparation, document workflows.

The classic mistake is starting with the most “strategic” process. Start with the most measurable one: the results of your first 90 days have to show up in the numbers, or you won't get backing for phase two.

Days 31–60: train the people who own the process

Tools aren't adopted by IT departments — they're adopted by process owners. You need to train three levels at once: executives in making decisions and setting tasks for AI, middle managers in redesigning processes, and frontline staff in working with the tools day to day. One trained enthusiast won't change a company; a critical mass within one specific process will.

Format matters: not lectures, but hands-on work on the company's own tasks. Every employee should walk out of the training with a tool already set up inside their real workflow.

Days 61–90: measure the impact against a baseline

Before launch, lock in your baseline metrics: cycle time, cost per transaction, error rate, internal customer NPS. On day 90, compare. Honest measurement beats flattering measurement: even a modest −20% in cycle time on a single process is an argument for scaling — backed by your own data, not someone else's case study.

Three traps that derail the timeline

A pilot without an owner. If no specific executive with a KPI is accountable for the result, the project will quietly die. Technology ahead of the problem. “We need our own chatbot” is not a goal; the goal is “cut customer response time from 4 hours to 20 minutes.” Training after rollout. A tool without a trained team is just a license you keep paying for while nobody uses it.

What's next

Your first measured result is a foothold. From there, the cycle repeats: the next process, the next wave of training, the next measurement.
